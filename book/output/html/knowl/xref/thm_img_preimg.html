Theorem 10.3.6.

Let \(f: A \to B\text{,}\) and let \(C \subseteq A\) and \(D \subseteq B\text{.}\) Further, let \(C_1, C_2\) be subsets of \(A\) and let \(D_1, D_2\) be subsets of \(B\text{.}\) The following are true
  1. \(\displaystyle C \subseteq f^{-1}( f(C) )\)
    🔗
  2. \(\displaystyle f( f^{-1}(D) ) \subseteq D\)
    🔗
  3. \(f(C_1 \cap C_2) \subseteq f(C_1) \cap f(C_2)\) — note: need not be equal
    🔗
  4. \(f(C_1 \cup C_2) = f(C_1) \cup f(C_2)\) — note: are equal
    🔗
  5. \(\displaystyle f^{-1}(D_1 \cap D_2) = f^{-1}(D_1) \cap f^{-1}(D_2)\)
    🔗
  6. \(\displaystyle f^{-1}(D_1 \cup D_2) = f^{-1}(D_1) \cup f^{-1}(D_2)\)
    🔗
