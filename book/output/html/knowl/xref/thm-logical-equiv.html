Theorem 4.2.3. Logical equivalences.

Let \(P, Q\) and \(R\) be statements. Then
  • Implication: \((P \implies Q) \equiv ( (\neg P)\lor Q )\)
    🔗
  • Contrapositive: \((P\implies Q) \equiv ((\neg Q) \implies (\neg P))\)
    🔗
  • Biconditional: \((P \iff Q) \equiv ( (P\implies Q) \land (Q\implies P))\)
    🔗
  • Double negation: \(\neg{(\neg{(P)})} \equiv P\)
    🔗
  • Commutative laws
    • \(\displaystyle P \lor Q \equiv Q \lor P\)
      🔗
    • \(\displaystyle P \land Q \equiv Q \land P\)
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗
  • Associative laws
    • \(\displaystyle P \lor (Q\lor R) \equiv (P \lor Q)\lor R\)
      🔗
    • \(\displaystyle P \land (Q\land R) \equiv (P \land Q)\land R\)
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗
  • Distributive laws
    • \(P \lor (Q \land R) \equiv (P \lor Q) \land (P \lor R)\text{.}\)
      🔗
    • \(P \land (Q \lor R) \equiv (P \land Q) \lor (P \land R)\text{.}\)
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗
  • DeMorgan’s laws
    • \(\displaystyle \neg{(P \lor Q)} \equiv (\neg{P}) \land (\neg{Q})\)
      🔗
    • \(\displaystyle \neg{(P \land Q)} \equiv (\neg{P}) \lor (\neg{Q})\)
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
🔗

Proof.
These can all be proved in a straightforward, but slightly tedious, manner by computing and comparing truth tables.
