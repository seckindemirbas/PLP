Lemma 6.5.5.

Let \(a \in \mathbb{R}\) and let \((x_n)\) be a sequence that converges to \(a\text{.}\) Then there is some \(N \in \mathbb{N}\) so that for all \(n \in \mathbb{N}\text{,}\) when \(n \gt N\text{,}\) we have
\begin{equation*} \frac{|a|}{2} \leq |x_n| \leq \frac{3|a|}{2}. \end{equation*}
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Let \(a\) and \((x_n)\) be as given, and let \(\epsilon = \frac{|a|}{2}\text{.}\) Then since \(x_n \to a\text{,}\) we know that there is \(N \in \mathbb{N}\) so that for all integer \(n \gt N\text{,}\)
\begin{equation*} |x_n - a| \lt \frac{|a|}{2}. \end{equation*}
The reverse triangle inequality, Corollary 5.4.7 then tells us that
\begin{equation*} |x_n-a| \geq \left||x_n| - |a| \right| \end{equation*}
and hence we know that
\begin{equation*} \left||x_n| - |a| \right| \lt \frac{|a|}{2} \end{equation*}
This is equivalent (see Lemma 5.4.5) to the statement
\begin{equation*} -\frac{|a|}{2} \lt |x_n|-|a| \lt \frac{|a|}{2} \end{equation*}
from which the result quickly follows by adding \(|a|\) to both sides.
