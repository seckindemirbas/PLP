Theorem 6.5.1. Basic properties of limits of sequences.

Let \((x_n)\) and \((y_n)\) be sequences so that
\begin{equation*} \lim_{n \to \infty} x_n = a \qquad \text{ and } \qquad \lim_{n \to \infty} y_n = b \end{equation*}
Additionally let \(c,d \in \mathbb{R}\text{.}\) Then
  1. The limit of a sequence is unique
    🔗
  2. Linearity of limits: \(\ds \lim _{n\to \infty }(c \cdot x_{n} + d \cdot y_{n})=c \cdot a+ d \cdot b \text{.}\)
    🔗
  3. Product of limits: \(\ds \lim_{n\to \infty} \left(x_n \cdot y_n\right) = a \cdot b\text{.}\)
    🔗
  4. Reciprocal of limit: \(\ds\lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{1}{y_n} = \frac{1}{b}\) as long as \(b\neq 0\)
    🔗
  5. Ratio of limits: \(\ds \lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{x_n}{y_n} = \frac{a}{b}\) as long as \(b\neq 0\)
    🔗
