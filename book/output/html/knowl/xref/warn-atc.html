Warning 2.5.3. A common logical error.

The false deduction
  • \(P\) implies \(Q\) is true, and
    🔗
  • \(Q\) is true,
    🔗
  • and hence \(P\) must be true
    🔗
is called affirming the consequent.
🔗
The flow of logic is wrong — check the truth table. Also notice that our arrow notation for the implication, \(\implies\text{,}\) helps to remind us that truth should flow from the hypothesis to the conclusion and not the other way around.
